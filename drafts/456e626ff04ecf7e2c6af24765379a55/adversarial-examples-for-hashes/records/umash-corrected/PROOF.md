# A corrected UMASH projection bound, and the high-valuation ENH-only case

## 1. Result and scope

Put

\[
q=2^{64},\qquad p=2^{61}-1,\qquad C=364816,\qquad
A=\frac{C}{q-561}.
\]

This document establishes the **corrected-bound outcome** of the task. For
every fixed 64-bit seed, every two distinct byte strings of length at most
`8L`, and every positive integer `L`, the literal primary UMASH construction
in the supplied reference/Lean model satisfies

\[
\boxed{\Pr[\operatorname{UMASH}_{64}(x)=\operatorname{UMASH}_{64}(y)]
 \le \frac{45635\,\lceil L/512\rceil}{2^{61}}.}                 \tag{1}
\]

Here the 34 OH words are sampled uniformly **without replacement**, and the
polynomial multiplier is independent and uniform on `{2,...,p-1}`. The same
upper bound holds with independent uniform OH words. No upper bound on `L`
is required. As usual an upper bound may be clipped at one.

A more precise envelope, also proved here, is

\[
\Pr[\text{primary collision}]
\le A+(1-A)\min\left(1,\frac{2\lceil L/32\rceil}{p-2}\right). \tag{2}
\]

For two short inputs (both at most eight bytes), use instead
`1/(q-561)`; equal-length distinct short inputs never collide.

The principal new ingredient is a count for the implemented ENH fold,
valid with **any fixed common XOR mask**, including zero:

\[
\Pr[\text{primary projected ENH-only collision}]
\le \frac{5542}{q}                                           \tag{3}
\]

whenever at least one of the final data words changes. This count does not
grow with the valuation of the change. It needs no character sums, LP
relaxation, extremality conjecture, or assumption that a common PH product
is uniform.

For the primary and secondary compressors jointly, when all nonfinal
chunks agree and the final data differ, we prove

\[
\boxed{\Pr[\text{both projected compressors collide}]
\le \frac{4721784}{q^2}<2^{-105}<2^{-87}.}                    \tag{4}
\]

The probability in (4) first uses IID OH words, exactly as in
`OpenENHOnly`. Dividing by `1-561/q` still leaves it below `2^-105`.
In particular, **`OpenENHOnly` is proved**, including every `32<=r<=63`.
The theorem also covers the other ENH-only valuations and one-word changes.

Equation (1) is weaker than the published 55-bit envelope, by the exact
factor `45635/64`. It is an upper bound, not an attained collision rate.
**Neither `SharpPrimaryProjection` nor `OpenPHENH` is asserted here.**
The published 55-bit and 83-bit claims are neither proved nor refuted by
this document. The production fingerprint inherits (1) by inclusion of its
collision event in the primary event; this is a linear corrected envelope,
not the published quadratic 83-bit bound.

All counts and rational comparisons used below are regenerated by
[`checks/run_xeon.sh`](checks/run_xeon.sh). The proof is a natural-language,
computer-assisted proof, ready to be translated into Lean; it is not
presented as an already checked Lean proof. It is self-contained: none of
the unformalized probability propositions in the supplied ledger is used
as a premise. The ideal key model is the one specified above, not a claim
about the information-theoretic distribution of a short Salsa20 seed.

## 2. Literal arithmetic and probabilistic model

A word is an integer in `[0,q)`. XOR and AND operate on its binary digits.
The notation `a odot b` denotes unreduced multiplication of the two word
polynomials in `F_2[X]`, interpreted as a 128-bit integer. It has degree at
most 126. Write a 128-bit value as `(low,high)`.

For one PH chunk and its two independent uniform key words,

\[
\operatorname{PH}(a,b;k,l)=(a\mathbin\oplus k)\odot(b\mathbin\oplus l).
\]

For the final chunk, put

\[
B_0=(a+k)\bmod q,\quad B_1=(b+l)\bmod q,\quad
B_0B_1=qH+L,
\]

and, for the fixed word tag `tau`, define

\[
U=(H+\tau)\bmod q,\qquad E_\tau=(L,U\mathbin\oplus L).       \tag{5}
\]

Thus the high word really does include the low word by XOR. All products
in (5) are ordinary unsigned integer products. In particular this is not
an analysis of the displayed, unfolded NH formula alone.

A block has `n` chunks, `1<=n<=16`, and original byte size `b` with
`1<=b<=256` and `n=ceil(b/16)`. Its tag is
`tau=seed XOR (b mod 256)`. The primary compressor XORs the first `n-1`
PH products and the final ENH value. The secondary compressor XORs:

* fixed lane-wise linear shuffles of the first `n-1` PH products;
* the same, unshuffled final ENH value;
* a PH product of the checksum with the two extra twisting keys.

The checksum is the XOR of the `n` data chunks and their `n` original key
pairs. The twisting keys are independent of all 32 original chunk-key
words in the IID model. The exact shufflers will not be needed: in the
joint case proved here, every shuffled PH term is common to the messages.

Projection means

\[
\mathcal R(L,H)=(L\bmod p,H\bmod p).
\]

Throughout the block arguments, messages, tags, and seed are fixed before
sampling the keys. Conditioning and independence arguments are first
performed for IID words. Section 9 transfers the bounds to distinct words.

## 3. Congruence masks and their exact certificate

The next definitions and proofs work for any `w>=1`, `q=2^w`, and positive
modulus `p`. Let `m0=floor((q-1)/p)` and define

\[
\begin{split}
D&=\{x\oplus y:0\le x,y<q,\ x\equiv y\pmod p\},\\
P(d)&=\{(d+jp)/2:-m_0\le j\le m_0,\ d+jp\ge0,\ d+jp\text{ even},\
       ((d+jp)/2)\mathbin\&d=(d+jp)/2\}.
\end{split}                                                   \tag{6}
\]

**Lemma 3.1 (exact pattern condition).** If `d=x XOR y`, then

\[
x-y=2(x\mathbin\&d)-d.
\]

Consequently `x congruent y mod p` if and only if `x & d` belongs to
`P(d)`. In particular `d` must belong to `D`.

**Proof.** Equal bit positions cancel in integer subtraction. Each differing
bit contributes its positive weight if it is set in `x`, and its negative
weight otherwise. The difference is a multiple `jp` with `|j|<=m0`
exactly under (6). Conversely a submask `t` in (6) is realized by
`x=t`, `y=d XOR t`. Both words are in `[0,q)`. Adding arbitrary equal
bits outside `d` gives every other realization. ∎

Here is a complete finite procedure for computing `D`. Let `S(n,w)` be
the support masks of signed binary representations of the integer `n`
with `w` digits in `{-1,0,1}`. Use the following recursion:

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
S(0,0)=\{0\},&S(n,0)=\varnothing\quad(n\ne0),\\
S(n,w)=\varnothing&\text{if }|n|\ge2^w,\\
S(n,w)=\{2s:s\in S(n/2,w-1)\}&\text{if }n\text{ is even},\\
S(n,w)=\{2s+1:s\in S((n-1)/2,w-1)\cup S((n+1)/2,w-1)\}
 &\text{if }n\text{ is odd}.
\end{array}                                                    \tag{7}
\]

The base cases precede the recursive cases. For positive width, (7)
inspects the possible least significant digit, then removes that digit
and divides by two. This proves soundness and completeness by induction
on `w`. Negating all digits preserves support. Lemma 3.1 therefore gives

\[
D=\bigcup_{j=0}^{m_0}S(jp,w).                                  \tag{8}
\]

At the actual parameters `m0=8`. Evaluating (6)--(8) with exact integers
gives the following complete statistics. The full masks and their
patterns are in [`triangular.json`](checks/triangular.json).

| Quantity | Exact value |
|---|---:|
| `|D|` | 852 |
| Counts in `S(jp,64)`, `j=0,...,8`, before union | 1, 184, 123, 361, 62, 355, 121, 178, 1 |
| Masks with respectively 1, 2, 4, 8 patterns | 1, 435, 357, 59 |
| `T_0 = sum_(d in D) |P(d)|` | 2771 |
| Masks of low-bit value 0, 1 | 248, 604 |
| Masks of high-bit value 0, 1 | 248, 604 |

For `N_r=|D intersect 2^r Z|` and
`T_r=sum_(d in D, 2^r divides d) |P(d)|`, the further counts are

| r | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | every r>=4 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| N_r | 852 | 248 | 64 | 2 | 1 |
| T_r | 2771 | 615 | 127 | 3 | 1 |

These small finite computations are proof certificates, not sampled key
frequencies. [`certify_constants.py`](checks/certify_constants.py) also
reconstructs `D` independently by an addition automaton. For each `j`,
it branches over input bits in `y=x+jp`, retaining `(carry,partial XOR)`;
it accepts exactly paths with final carry zero. Every accepted path is a
real pair, and every nonoverflowing pair follows a path. Thus this second
enumeration is independently sound and complete. It agrees with (8).

Two facts used below do not require computation:

**Lemma 3.2.** At the actual parameters,
`D intersect 16Z={0}`, and every nonzero mask has highest set bit at least 60.

**Proof.** If 16 divides `x XOR y`, their low four bits agree, so 16
divides `x-y=jp`. Since `p` is odd and `|j|<=8`, this forces `j=0` and
`x=y`. For a nonzero mask, `x!=y`, and
`p<=|x-y|<=x XOR y`; since `p>=2^60`, its top bit is at least 60. ∎

## 4. ENH with a common XOR mask: the new count

Fix any two distinct ENH data pairs, any two tags, and any common pair of
XOR offsets `(M_low,M_high)`. Translate the first input by its random keys,
and denote its operands by independent uniform `A,B` in `[0,q)`. There
are fixed increments `delta,epsilon` modulo `q` such that

\[
A'=(A+\delta)\bmod q,\qquad B'=(B+\epsilon)\bmod q.             \tag{9}
\]

At least one increment is nonzero. Exchange operands if necessary so
`delta!=0` and

\[
r=v_2(\delta)\le v_2(\epsilon),\quad R=2^r,
\]

where a zero increment has valuation `w` for this comparison. Then
`0<=r<w`. Integer differences modulo `q` and XOR differences between
distinct words have the same valuation: at the lowest differing bit their
integer difference is `2^r` times an odd integer. This identifies `r`
with the `enhValuation` in the two-word Lean obligation.

Write `AB=qH+L`, `A'B'=qH'+L'`, and
`U=(H+tau) mod q`, `U'=(H'+tau') mod q`. The primary words under
consideration are

\[
X=(M_{\rm low}\oplus L,\ M_{\rm high}\oplus L\oplus U),
\quad
Y=(M_{\rm low}\oplus L',\ M_{\rm high}\oplus L'\oplus U').    \tag{10}
\]

### 4.1 Small valuations

**Lemma 4.1 (low additive difference).** The random variable
`(L'-L) mod q` is uniform on the multiples of `R`, with probability
`R/q` at every such value.

**Proof.** Modulo `q`, it equals
`delta B + epsilon A + delta epsilon`. For fixed `A`, multiplication by
`delta=R a`, `a` odd, maps the `q` possibilities for `B` onto the
`q/R` multiples of `R`, with exactly `R` preimages each. The other two
terms are multiples of `R`, so their addition permutes that image. ∎

**Lemma 4.2 (low projection).** The probability that the low words in
(10) are congruent modulo `p` is at most `R T_r/q`.

**Proof.** Their XOR mask is `d=L XOR L'`. The low products agree modulo
`R`, so `R` divides `d`. On projection equality, Lemma 3.1 gives a
pattern `t=(M_low XOR L)&d` in `P(d)`. It fixes

\[
L\mathbin\&d=(M_{\rm low}\mathbin\&d)\oplus t,
\quad
L'-L=d-2((M_{\rm low}\mathbin\&d)\oplus t).                  \tag{11}
\]

Every one of the `T_r` possible `(d,t)` pairs therefore specifies one
additive target modulo `q`. Each has probability at most `R/q` by
Lemma 4.1. The union bound is valid even when different pairs give the
same target. ∎

For `r=0,1,2,3`, the four primary upper numerators obtained this way are

\[
2771,\quad1230,\quad508,\quad24.                              \tag{12}
\]

### 4.2 Low equality and the high projection

**Lemma 4.3 (a single lifted fibre).** In (9)--(10), fix `A`, a wrap
indicator `beta` in `{0,1}` for `B+epsilon`, a mask `m` in `D`, and
a pattern `t` in `P(m)`. There is at most one `B` in `[0,q)` satisfying
all of

1. `floor((B+epsilon)/q)=beta`;
2. `L=L'`;
3. the high words of (10) have XOR mask `m` and first-word pattern `t`.

**Proof.** Put `Q=q/R`, `alpha=floor((A+delta)/q)`, and

\[
d=(\delta-\alpha q)/R,\qquad e=(\epsilon-\beta q)/R.
\]

For the fixed `A,beta`, these are fixed signed integers, `d` is odd
(subtracting `alpha*Q` preserves oddness because `Q` is even),
and `A'=A+Rd`, `B'=B+Re`. Thus

\[
A'B'-AB=R(dB+eA+Rde).                                        \tag{13}
\]

Because `L=L'`, the high XOR mask in (10) is also `U XOR U'=m`.
The specified high projection pattern gives

\[
U\mathbin\&m=z(B):=((AB)\mathbin\&m)
                    \oplus(M_{\rm high}\mathbin\&m)\oplus t. \tag{14}
\]

Here masking `AB` by the word mask `m` is the same as masking `L`.
The signed difference identity gives `U'-U=m-2z(B)`. Since
`U'-U congruent H'-H+(tau'-tau) mod q`, equation (13) and low equality
imply, first modulo `q^2` and then after dividing by `R`,

\[
dB+eA+Rde\equiv Q(m-2z(B)-(\tau'-\tau))\pmod {qQ}.
\]

In particular the following weaker congruence is necessary:

\[
dB+eA+Rde\equiv Q(m-2z(B)-(\tau'-\tau))\pmod q.              \tag{15}
\]

Suppose two distinct words `B_1,B_2` satisfy (15). Let
`j=v2(B_1-B_2)<w`. Multiplication, AND with a fixed mask, and XOR with
a fixed word all preserve agreement of the low `j` bits. Therefore
`z(B_1) congruent z(B_2) mod 2^j`. Subtracting (15) yields

\[
d(B_1-B_2)\equiv-2Q(z(B_1)-z(B_2))\pmod q.                  \tag{16}
\]

The left side has valuation exactly `j`, because `d` is odd. The right
side is divisible by `2^(j+1)`. Since `j<w`, this contradicts (16)
modulo `2^(j+1)`. There can be at most one `B`. The wrap restriction and
the full modulus `qQ` can only reject this candidate. ∎

For an explicitly constructive version, decide the bits of `B` from low
to high in (15). The coefficient of its next bit on the left is one,
while the varying term on the right uses strictly earlier bits. The
function `solve_lift` in `checks/triangular.py` implements that procedure.
The proof requires at most one solution, not that every candidate solves
the original equations.

**Lemma 4.4 (low-equal high projection).** With arbitrary tags and arbitrary
fixed common offsets, the probability of `L=L'` and equality of the high
residues in (10) is at most

\[
\frac{2}{q}\sum_{m\in D}|P(m)|=\frac{5542}{q}.               \tag{17}
\]

**Proof.** There are `q` choices for `A`, two possible values of `beta`,
and `T_0=2771` mask-pattern pairs. Lemma 4.3 permits at most one `B`
for each combination. There are exactly `q^2` equally likely operand
pairs. Divide the upper count `2qT_0` by `q^2`. This includes zero
operands, zero mask, input wraps, tag wraps, and unequal tags. ∎

**Theorem 4.5 (all ENH-only primary cases).** If two same-count blocks
have identical nonfinal chunks and different final data, their primary
projected collision probability under IID keys is at most `5542/q`.
For minimum valuation `r<=3`, the constants (12) also apply.

**Proof.** Condition on every PH key. Their common output is a fixed
`M` independent of the remaining two ENH keys, so (10) applies. For
`r<=3`, use Lemma 4.2 and (12). For `r>=4`, low products agree modulo
16. A projected low collision forces `L=L'` by Lemma 3.2, even after
the common XOR mask. Then use Lemma 4.4. The four numerators in (12)
are all below 5542. Average over the conditioned keys. The argument
allows no PH chunks at all. ∎

This is where using the implemented fold is essential. Equation (14)
turns the high sign choices into a function of low product bits. Those
bits cannot cancel the lowest differing bit of `B` in (16). Counting
all high sign choices independently would discard this fact.

## 5. PH differences and the improved generic primary count

**Lemma 5.1 (full PH difference).** For two different PH input pairs,
every prescribed 128-bit XOR difference of their outputs has probability
at most `1/q` under the two IID keys.

**Proof.** Choose a data coordinate that differs, and leave the opposite
key word `v` free after conditioning the other key. Expanding binary
polynomial multiplication, the difference is `d odot v XOR c`, where
`d` is a fixed nonzero word polynomial. Multiplication by `d` is
injective: a product of two nonzero binary polynomials has nonzero
leading coefficient. Thus each target has at most one free word. ∎

**Lemma 5.2 (odd difference).** If a differing PH coordinate is odd as
an XOR difference, the complete primary block projection collides with
probability at most `17/q`, regardless of changes in the other chunks
or tag.

**Proof.** Leave its opposite key word free and condition all other
keys. The two low outputs are affine binary-polynomial products in `v`
plus fixed XOR offsets. Bit `j` of each depends only on `v_0,...,v_j`;
bit `j` of their XOR has coefficient one on `v_j`, because the
coefficient difference is odd. For each fixed signed multiple `kp`,
`-8<=k<=8`, consider the integer low-word difference modulo `q`.
After the lower bits of `v` are known, the borrow into subtraction bit
`j` is known. The required bit of `kp` then determines `v_j` uniquely.
There is at most one word for each of the 17 targets. Actual integer
equality and the high projection can only reject these candidates. ∎

**Lemma 5.3 (even difference).** If the chosen nonzero PH multiplying
difference is even, a primary projected block collision has probability
at most `604^2/q=364816/q`.

**Proof.** As in Lemma 5.1, the complete raw XOR difference is an
injective affine function `d odot v XOR c` after all other keys are
conditioned. Its bit 0 is fixed, because `d` is even. Its bit 127 is
fixed, because a word-by-word carryless product has degree at most 126.
Every projected collision must have both lane masks in `D`.

For either prescribed bit 0, at most 604 low masks are possible; for
either prescribed bit 63 of the high lane, at most 604 high masks are
possible, by the exact table in Section 3. There are at most `604^2`
candidate full XOR targets, and injectivity allows at most one free key
word per target. Divide by `q`, then average the conditioning. ∎

Consequently, whenever any PH chunk differs, the bound `C/q` holds:
use Lemma 5.2 if a differing coordinate is odd, and Lemma 5.3 otherwise.
The bit-0 and bit-127 restrictions are both necessary conditions, not
claims that all counted targets are attainable.

## 6. Tags, point masses, and the exhaustive block ledger

**Lemma 6.1 (high product point mass).** For independent uniform words
`A,B`, any prescribed `h` satisfies

\[
\Pr[\lfloor AB/q\rfloor=h]<66/q.
\]

**Proof.** The case `A=0` contributes at most `q` pairs. For each
`A>0`, the half-open interval `qh<=AB<q(h+1)` contains at most
`q/A+1` integers `B`. Thus there are at most
`q(2+sum_(A=1)^(q-1) 1/A)-1` pairs. Split the harmonic sum into the
64 intervals `[2^j,2^(j+1)-1]`. Each contains `2^j` summands at most
`2^-j`, so the sum is at most 64. The count is less than `66q`. ∎

**Lemma 6.2 (tag-only comparison).** If all expanded chunks agree but
the two valid block tags differ, primary projection collision has
probability less than `269280/q`.

**Proof.** The tag difference `d=tau'-tau` is a nonzero signed integer
of magnitude at most 255: the tags share their upper 56 bits. The low
words are identical. Their high XOR difference is
`(H+tau mod q) XOR (H+tau' mod q)`, independently of the common PH
offset and common low fold. By Lemma 3.2 a projected high collision
requires a differing bit numbered at least 60. Hence moving by the
fixed signed step `d` on the word circle crosses a boundary at a
multiple of `2^60`. There are 16 boundaries, and exactly `|d|`
starting words per boundary in that fixed direction. Their sets are
disjoint since `|d|<2^60`. Translating by `tau` leaves at most
`16|d|<=4080` values of `H`. Lemma 6.1 bounds their total probability
by less than `4080*66/q=269280/q`. ∎

**Lemma 6.3 (projected point mass).** A final ENH value with independent
uniform operands, after any fixed tag and fixed XOR offset, has
probability at most `(82q-81)/q^2<82/q` of any prescribed projected
pair.

**Proof.** Each word residue has at most nine representatives, since
`q=8p+8`. Hence a projected pair has at most 81 raw representatives.
Undoing the XOR offset, triangular fold, and tag is bijective on all
128-bit words. At most one inverse target is integer product zero,
with `2q-1` operand pairs. A nonzero product has at most `q-1`
operand pairs: for each nonzero first operand, at most one second
operand works. The total is therefore at most
`(2q-1)+80(q-1)=82q-81`. ∎

**Theorem 6.4 (complete primary block bound).** For any two valid blocks
whose chunk lists or tags differ, the primary projected collision
probability under IID OH words is at most `C/q=364816/q`.

**Proof by exhaustive cases.**

| Block comparison | Bound | Reason |
|---|---:|---|
| Different chunk counts | `<82/q` | Condition everything except the longer block's final key pair. It is unused by the shorter block; apply Lemma 6.3. |
| Same counts; some PH chunk differs; an odd differing coordinate is selected | `<=17/q` | Lemma 5.2. |
| Same counts; some PH chunk differs; all differing coordinates are even | `<=364816/q` | Lemma 5.3. |
| Same counts; all PH chunks agree; final data differ | `<=5542/q` | Theorem 4.5, for all valuations and arbitrary tags. |
| Same expanded chunks; different tags | `<269280/q` | Lemma 6.2. |

If counts, chunks, and tags all agree, the block tuples agree and the
theorem's hypothesis excludes the pair. Thus the table is exhaustive.
Every displayed numerator is at most `C`. ∎

## 7. Joint ENH-only theorem and `OpenENHOnly`

**Lemma 7.1 (low PH difference distribution).** Suppose two PH input
pairs have coordinate XOR differences divisible by `R=2^r`, and at
least one has valuation exactly `r<w`. Conditional on either companion
key that leaves such a coefficient multiplying the other free key, the
low XOR difference is uniform on the multiples of `R`.

**Proof.** In binary-polynomial notation the multiplying coefficient is
`X^r g`, with `g(0)=1`. Multiplication by `g` modulo `X^(w-r)` is
invertible, as its coefficients determine an inverse successively.
Thus the truncated multiplication has image precisely the multiples
of `R` and exactly `R` preimages per image value. The affine offset
also has its first `r` bits zero: the two input pairs agree in those
bits, so their low products do. Addition by that offset preserves the
image and uniformity. ∎

**Theorem 7.2.** Let two valid blocks have the same chunk count and
identical nonfinal chunks, and suppose their final data differ. With
arbitrary fixed tags, the IID joint projected probability is at most
`4721784/q^2`.

**Proof.** Let `r` be the minimum valuation of the nonzero final-word
increments. The checksum inputs differ by exactly the XOR of the two
final data chunks: the common data and original key pairs cancel.
Thus this difference is nonzero and has the same minimum valuation
`r`. Condition on all original chunk keys. The two twisting keys
remain independent and uniform. The original primary event is now
fixed, as is the final ENH XOR difference `Delta_E`.

If `r<=3`, Lemma 7.1 says that the checksum PH low difference is
uniform on the multiples of `R`. The low ENH difference also belongs
to that subspace. Their XOR is the secondary low difference, because
all other secondary terms are common. The conditional secondary low
projection probability is at most `N_r R/q`. The primary probability
is at most `R T_r/q` by Lemma 4.2. Multiplying this conditional bound
and then averaging gives

\[
\Pr[\text{joint}]\le\frac{R^2T_rN_r}{q^2}.
\]

For `r=0,1,2,3` the numerators are respectively
`2360892,610080,130048,384`, all below 4721784.

If `r>=4`, a primary projected collision forces raw ENH low equality
by Lemma 3.2. The checksum PH low difference is divisible by `R`, so
a secondary projected collision forces that low difference to be zero
as well. The secondary high difference must belong to `D`. Therefore
the full checksum PH difference must lie in the set

\[
\{(0,v\oplus\Delta_{E,\rm high}):v\in D\},
\]

which has 852 elements. Each has conditional probability at most
`1/q` by Lemma 5.1. The conditional secondary bound is `852/q`,
uniform over all original keys satisfying the primary event. Theorem
4.5 bounds that primary event by `5542/q`. Therefore

\[
\Pr[\text{joint}]\le\frac{5542\cdot852}{q^2}
=\frac{4721784}{q^2}<2^{-105}.
\]

The multiplication is of a primary probability and a uniform conditional
secondary bound; no independence of completed compressors is assumed. ∎

For the exact Lean proposition `OpenENHOnly`, same count and
`phDiffCount=0` imply equality of the two `dropLast` chunk lists.
`enhChanges=2` supplies nonzero increments in both coordinates, and
their minimum valuation agrees with `enhValuation` by the observation
after (9). Its `32<=r<=63` hypothesis is contained in Theorem 7.2.
Finally `4721784<2^23<2^41`, so the required strict `<2^-87` follows.
The fixed seed and validity hypotheses give tags allowed by the theorem.
This checks every hypothesis of that closed proposition.

## 8. Encoding, short messages, and polynomial identity

The following details are included so the corrected theorem does not
depend on an unproved assembly premise.

### 8.1 Encoding distinguishes long messages

For a message of byte length `s>8`, the reference emits
`ceil(s/16)` expanded 16-byte chunks and groups them into
`ceil(s/256)` blocks. If `9<=s<16`, its one chunk consists of the
first and last eight bytes. Otherwise it emits ordinary full 16-byte
chunks, with any final short piece represented by the last 16 bytes
of the whole message. Only the last block can have byte size below
256. All block sizes are positive and at most 256.

**Lemma 8.1.** For a fixed seed, the ordered list of expanded chunks
and block tags determines every message of length greater than eight.

**Proof.** The block count and last tag determine `s`: XORing the
tag with the seed gives the last block's size modulo 256, whose
unique representative in `[1,256]` is its size. All previous blocks
have size 256. For `s<16`, the first and last eight-byte intervals
cover the string and recover it. For `s>=16`, the ordinary full
chunks recover its entire full-chunk prefix; when there is a remainder,
the overlapping final chunk recovers that remainder. Their locations
are known from `s`. Thus every byte is determined. ∎

In particular distinct long messages with equal block counts have a
differing aligned block tuple, chosen independently of the keys.

### 8.2 The two reversible branches

**Lemma 8.2 (short branch).** Under IID OH words, the hash of any
fixed short message is uniform on words. Distinct short messages of
equal length never collide, and those of different lengths collide
with probability exactly `1/q`.

**Proof.** The short packing is injective for each length. At lengths
4 through 8, let `lo` and `hi` be the little-endian reads of the first
and last four bytes. At lengths below 4, let `lo` be the first byte
if the length is odd, and zero otherwise; let `hi` be the little-endian
read of the last two bytes if the length is at least two, and zero
otherwise. The packed word is

```
hi * 2^32 + ((hi + lo) mod 2^32).
```

Its high half recovers `hi`; subtraction in its low half recovers `lo`.
The indicated reads cover the whole message in each case. Length zero
has just one message.

For clarity the complete short mixer, with all multiplications and the
noise addition modulo `q`, is

```
h = packed_word
h = h XOR (h >> 30)
h = h * 0xBF58476D1CE4E5B9
h = h XOR (h >> 27) XOR ((seed + k_length) mod q)
h = h * 0x94D049BB133111EB
h = h XOR (h >> 31).
```

Each XOR-right-shift is invertible by recovering bits from high to
low. Each multiplication constant in the short mixer is odd and
therefore invertible modulo `q`. The injected word is
`(seed+k_length) mod q`, which is uniform. XORing it into the
partially mixed message and performing the remaining permutations
makes the result uniform. At a fixed length the whole construction
is a permutation of the packed value. At two different lengths the
noise words are independent; their final hashes are independent
uniform words, proving the stated probability. ∎

**Lemma 8.3 (long finalizer).** The map
`F(x)=x XOR rotateLeft(x,8) XOR rotateLeft(x,33)` is invertible.

**Proof.** Let `R` be one-bit rotation as a linear operator over
`F_2`, so `R^64=I`. Then `N=R+I` has `N^64=0`. The polynomial
`1+z^8+z^33` has value one at `z=1`, so `F=I+N Q(N)` for a
polynomial `Q`. Put `J=NQ(N)`; it has `J^64=0`. The finite sum
`I+J+...+J^63` is the inverse of `I+J`. ∎

### 8.3 Formal coefficient polynomials

For the projected primary block outputs `(a_i,b_i)`, define the field
polynomial by starting at zero and updating

\[
P\longmapsto f^2(P+a_i)+f b_i.
\]

With `n` blocks its degree is at most `2n`, its constant term is
zero, and its first block supplies the coefficients of degrees
`2n` and `2n-1`. These assertions follow immediately by induction
on the number of updates. The implementation reduces the stored
accumulator modulo `q-8=8p` and uses `f^2 mod p`; reducing that
recurrence modulo `p` gives exactly this polynomial evaluation.

When one message is short, subtract from the long polynomial the
constant obtained by applying `F^-1` to the short result and then
reducing modulo `p`. The constant is independent of `f`, although
it may depend on OH keys. For two long messages subtract their
polynomials. By Lemma 8.3 and the congruent recurrence, full hash
equality implies that this comparison polynomial evaluates to zero.

**Lemma 8.4 (identity probability when at least one message is long).**
For distinct messages, under IID OH keys, the probability that this
comparison polynomial is identically zero is at most `C/q`.

**Proof by exhaustive cases.**

* For two long messages with equal block counts, use Lemma 8.1 to
  select a differing block tuple. Polynomial identity implies equality
  of its two projected coefficients. Theorem 6.4 gives `C/q`.
* For two long messages with different block counts, identity forces
  the two coefficients from the first block of the longer message to
  vanish: their degrees exceed the degree of the shorter polynomial.
  Condition every key except the final ENH pair in that first block.
  Lemma 6.3 gives `<82/q`. Other blocks may reuse these keys; the
  necessary event on this one block is sufficient for the bound.
* For one short and one long message, identity forces the short
  constant to be zero, because the long polynomial has no constant
  term. Lemmas 8.2 and 8.3 make its unreduced value a uniform word.
  Precisely nine words in `[0,q)` have residue zero modulo `p`,
  namely `0,p,...,8p`. The probability is `9/q`. Shared keys with
  the long computation do not matter: this is a necessary event,
  not an independence argument.

All these bounds are at most `C/q`. ∎

This lemma deliberately treats comparisons with at least one long
message. For two short messages the actual collision estimate in Lemma
8.2 is used directly. No claim about the reduced inverse-finalized
short/short **constant-polynomial identity** is needed or inferred.
In particular this argument does not silently assert the stronger
all-message premise `SharpPrimaryProjection` from the supplied Lean file.

## 9. Distinct-key conditioning and the full hash

**Lemma 9.1.** For 34 independent uniform words, the probability of
all being distinct is at least `1-561/q>0`. An IID key-event bound
`b` becomes at most `b/(1-561/q)` under uniform distinct sampling.

**Proof.** There are `binom(34,2)=561` pairs; each repeats with
probability `1/q`. The union bound gives the acceptance estimate.
Every injective ordered tuple has equal IID probability, so conditioning
on acceptance is uniform distinct sampling. For any event `E`,
`Pr(E|accept)<=Pr(E)/Pr(accept)`. ∎

This transfers Lemma 8.4 to `A=C/(q-561)` and the short bound to
`1/(q-561)`. It transfers (4) to
`4721784/(q(q-561))<2^-105`. The independently sampled multiplier
remains independent and uniform after conditioning OH words.

**Lemma 9.2 (polynomial root bound).** A nonzero polynomial of degree
at most `d` over a field has at most `d` roots.

**Proof.** If it has a root `a`, division by `X-a` factors off that
root, lowering the degree by one. Every other root is a root of the
quotient, since its value of `X-a` is nonzero. Induction on degree
proves the statement; a nonzero constant has no roots. ∎

The primality of `p` is independently certified as described in Section
10. Messages of length at most `8L` have at most `ceil(L/32)` blocks.
Thus every comparison above has degree at most `d=2ceil(L/32)`.
Conditional on an OH key with nonzero comparison polynomial, a uniform
multiplier in a set of size `p-2` gives collision probability at most
`rho=min(1,d/(p-2))` by Lemma 9.2. If `a` is the identity
probability, the total probability is at most `a+(1-a)rho`. Since
`a<=A<1` and `rho<=1`, this is at most `A+(1-A)rho`.

For two short messages Lemmas 8.2 and 9.1 give
`1/(q-561)<=A`, so (2) covers them too. This proves the envelope
for every message pair, with all cases enumerated.

Let `h=ceil(L/512)>=1`. Since `L<=512h`,
`ceil(L/32)<=16h`. Therefore (2) is at most

\[
A+\frac{32h}{p-2}
\le h\left(A+\frac{32}{p-2}\right)
<\frac{45635h}{2^{61}}.                                      \tag{18}
\]

The last comparison is exact rational arithmetic:

\[
2^{61}\left(\frac{364816}{2^{64}-561}
             +\frac{32}{2^{61}-3}\right)
=\frac{1941055691508763227645934840993350667993088}
       {42535295865117306584003665538960066195}
\in(45634,45635).
\]

This proves (1). Equations (1)--(2) also hold for IID OH words, since
their identity and short probabilities are no larger than the bounds
just used. A uniform multiplier set of size `p-1`, including 1, also
satisfies the same conservative root bound.

For a fingerprint, simultaneous equality implies equality of its first
component. Its first multiplier and OH keys have the marginal law just
analyzed, so its probability is at most (1). This inheritance uses no
independence between the completed components and proves no 83-bit
quadratic estimate.

## 10. Exact computations and certificate verification

The numerical part of the proof consists solely of finite integer and
rational calculations with explicit algorithms:

1. `triangular.py` evaluates recursion (7), patterns (6), all mask
   statistics used in the proof, and the rational rounding in (18).
   Its `solve_lift` also reconstructs candidate fibres bit by bit.
2. `certify_constants.py` independently computes the masks by the
   addition automaton, compares the complete lists and patterns, checks
   the table, verifies all stated numerical inequalities, and produces
   a primality certificate. Its small-width cross-checks enumerate
   **all** congruent pairs and patterns at each declared width/modulus.
3. `exhaustive_lift.cpp` is an independent literal enumeration of all
   increment pairs with the first nonzero increment of minimum
   valuation, all `A,B`, and three specified tag/common-mask profiles
   at widths 3 through 6. It checks (14)--(15), uniqueness of every
   actual `(A,beta,m,t)` fibre, and the constructive lifted candidate.
   This checks the new algebra; the general proof is Lemma 4.3, not
   an extrapolation from the enumerations.

For primality, `certify_constants.py` completely factors `p-1`:

\[
p-1=2\cdot3^2\cdot5^2\cdot7\cdot11\cdot13\cdot31\cdot41
       \cdot61\cdot151\cdot331\cdot1321.
\]

It proves each listed factor prime by trial division through its integer
square root, checks the product, and supplies the witness `g=37` such that

\[
g^{p-1}\equiv1\pmod p,\qquad
\gcd(g^{(p-1)/\ell}-1,p)=1
\quad\text{for each prime factor }\ell\text{ above}.          \tag{19}
\]

All residues, gcds, and the witness are recorded in `constants.json`.
For completeness, (19) proves primality as follows. For any prime divisor
`s` of `p`, the order of `g` modulo `s` divides `p-1`. If it were a
proper divisor, it would divide `(p-1)/ell` for some prime `ell`
above, contradicting the gcd condition. Its order is consequently
`p-1`, which divides `s-1` by the finite multiplicative group's
coset partition. Thus `s>=p`; as `s` divides `p`, equality follows.

The final run checks 35,779,968 operand-pair/profile visits in the independent

an exact production collision probability for an arbitrary message pair.

## 11. Formalization boundaries and remaining sharp questions

The dependency chain for the corrected primary theorem is

```
signed masks and patterns
  -> low additive count / triangular ENH fibre count
  -> all ENH-only primary cases
  -> PH odd/even cases + tag-only + fresh-product point mass
  -> complete block bound 364816/q
  -> encoding + unequal block counts + short/long + short/short
  -> distinct-key conditioning + polynomial roots
  -> corrected all-pairs bound (1).
```

The additional joint chain is

```
ENH primary count + independent twisting-key PH slice
  -> joint ENH-only count 4721784/q^2
  -> OpenENHOnly.
```

The crucial fibre lemma to formalize is Lemma 4.3: all variables are
finite words except the explicit signed wrap differences, and its
uniqueness proof is a single least-differing-bit contradiction. It
does not need a library theorem about NH differential universality.

The proof does not establish extremality of any family. It uses uniform
upper counts that cover every input pair, which is sufficient for the
corrected-bound option. In particular, an ENH mask count below 5542
and a PH count below 364816 would be needed to approach the published
constant. The PH+ENH equal-checksum problem does not reduce to Lemma
4.3 by conditioning a common mask: its PH masks differ between the
messages. Treating them as common would be an invalid extension.

Thus the original sharp primary proposition and `OpenPHENH` remain
open at the end of this corrected-bound proof. There is no claimed
counterexample, conditional or unconditional, to either published
headline.
