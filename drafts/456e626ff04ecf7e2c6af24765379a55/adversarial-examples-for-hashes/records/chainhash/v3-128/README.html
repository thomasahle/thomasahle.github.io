ChainHash-128 v3: measurement summary

ChainHash-128 v3: GCM field GF(2^128), eight interleaved comb blocks of 512 bytes per 4 KiB region, raw 256-bit CLNH accumulators, Horner in an independent y with the byte length leading, integer twist plus quintic finalizer. Output 128 bits; default block size 512 bytes. Key model: Model A 128 random bytes (s, y, c0..c4, tau); paper model 624 bytes (W+7 words, W=32). One function on every host: Horner stride, SIMD width, schoolbook or Karatsuba products, lazy or eager reduction, streaming chunks and region-aligned thread splits return the same digest; the SMHasher3 verification value is 0x1FCA728C for the 512-byte family (0x0F709CAD for the separately defined 256-byte family). Digests differ from the previous strided ChainHash-128. Specification: SPEC_v3_128.md; theorem: THEOREM_v3_128.md; project: thomasahle/chainhash.

Protocol

SMHasher3 --test=Speed with new scratch registrations linked against the unchanged baseline objects; average of alignments 0..7 at fixed 262144 bytes (smhasher3 average line); small keys: average of the 31 separately measured 1..31-byte costs. Xeon 8375C: two complete passes; higher fixed-size bulk and lower small average selected independently, pinned to cores 16-23. M2 Pro: three complete passes; median of bulk and median of small, independently; any run deviating more than 15 percent from its median is flagged, never dropped; every launch waited until no SMHasher3 process existed and the one-minute load was below 4.5. Key expansion is outside timing. Xeon B/TSC; M2 calibrated estimated cycles, not a privileged hardware cycle counter. Printed GiB/sec assumes 3.5 GHz.

The chart and Table 2 use the explicit chainhash128-v3.512school registration on both hosts: Xeon 14.43 B/cycle (schoolbook over Karatsuba +3.7%), M2 Pro 10.26 (+9.3%). Schoolbook products are therefore the dispatch target on XMM, ZMM and NEON; Karatsuba remains on YMM and in the portable evaluator. Against the previous strided ChainHash-128 in the same binaries the gain is 1.77× on the Xeon (four-lane VPCLMULQDQ on ZMM) and 1.019× on the M2, where the NEON loop is issue-bound rather than multiplier-bound. The public entry point on the M2 was first timed with a binary that still dispatched NEON to Karatsuba (9.41 B/cycle); those runs are retained as superseded in the JSON, and the row above is the rebuilt default. The Xeon public-default pass completed one of two passes.
