**Verdict: open.** The reduction step in UMASH's published proof still needs a general argument. I closed the cases involving different block counts, different chunk counts, and short versus long inputs, but some equal-length cases and the combined fingerprint remain unresolved. Exact small-word experiments found collision increases of about fourfold within the searched families, below the paper's roughly 64-fold estimate; these are not global maxima. I also counted the worst tested family exactly at 64 bits and proved that it satisfies the advertised primary-hash bound. Conversely, two examples reproduced through the production C code have 183 and 242 colliding choices when only one key word varies. They defeat a proposed proof shortcut with constant 128, but do not establish an attack when all keys are random. No production counterexample was found. `GAP.md` gives the proofs and remaining gaps; `toy/` contains reproducible code, exact results, and the search limitations.
